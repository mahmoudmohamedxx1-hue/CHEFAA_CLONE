## Clinical Pharmacology Insights

### Mechanism of Action Patterns

The extracted medications demonstrate several key pharmacological mechanisms:

1. **CNS-Based Muscle Relaxation**: Cyclobenzaprine and chlorzoxazone work through spinal cord pathways
2. **COX Enzyme Inhibition**: Diclofenac and ibuprofen block prostaglandin synthesis
3. **Mucolytic Actions**: Ambroxol, acetylcysteine, and erdosteine break down mucus glycoproteins
4. **Beta-1 Selective Blocking**: Ivabradine selectively inhibits cardiac pacemaker currents
5. **Proton Pump Inhibition**: Omeprazole irreversibly blocks gastric H+/K+-ATPase

### Dosing Regimens Analysis

**Standard Adult Dosages Identified**:
- Muscle relaxants: 5-10mg every 8 hours (cyclobenzaprine), 1-2 capsules every 8 hours (combination products)
- Mucolytics: 75mg 2-3 times daily (ambroxol), 600mg once daily (acetylcysteine)
- Antibiotics: 1 drop every 8 hours (eye drops), 1 tablet daily (oral moxifloxacin)
- Cardiovascular: 5-7.5mg twice daily (ivabradine), 1.5mg once daily (indapamide)

### Safety Profile Compilation

**Common Side Effect Patterns**:
- CNS medications: Drowsiness (up to 38% for cyclobenzaprine), dizziness, headache
- GI medications: Nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea (omeprazole), GI upset (mucolytics)
- Cardiovascular: Bradycardia (ivabradine), hypotension (indapamide)
- Topical: Skin irritation, localized burning sensation

**Contraindication Categories**:
- Cardiovascular: Heart block, severe heart failure (muscle relaxants)
- Respiratory: Asthma (acetylcysteine risk), severe COPD
- CNS: MAO inhibitor use (cyclobenzaprine)
- Pregnancy: Third trimester NSAIDs, first trimester mucolytics
